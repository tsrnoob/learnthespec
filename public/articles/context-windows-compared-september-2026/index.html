Context Windows, Compared Honestly, September 2026

Six models advertise a million tokens. A million tokens is not the same amount of text at each of them, the chat apps give you a fraction of it, and no model reasons reliably at full length.
Tsrnoob 4 SEPTEMBER 2026 COMPILED FROM 9 SOURCES CHECKED 4 SEP 2026

What is advertised

Start with the headline number, because it is the only one most comparisons print.

Context window — the total text a model holds at once, counting your input, its own reasoning and its output together. Max output is a separate, much smaller cap on how much it may write in one reply.

A million tokens is not a fixed amount of text

This is the part that breaks the comparison. Tokens are not words, and the conversion changes between models.

Anthropic publishes both figures for its own line-up.

“1M tokens is roughly 555k words or 2.5M Unicode characters on the current tokenizer… models before it fit about 750k words in 1M tokens.”1

Same advertised window, nearly 200,000 fewer words, because the tokenizer changed. Two vendors both claiming a million tokens are not offering the same capacity, and only one of them tells you so.

Tokenizer — the rule that chops text into tokens. A tokenizer that produces more tokens per word fills the same window with less text. Vendors publish window sizes in tokens, so the comparable figure is words, and almost nobody publishes it.

The app is not the API

The same company sells a million-token model and hands you a fraction of it in the chat window.

OpenAI's plan comparison lists a total context window of 27K for GPT Instant and describes the usable share as “~12 pages of text”. For its reasoning models the same row reads “Varies”.2

Its own footnote explains why the usable part is smaller still.

“The portion available for user input is smaller than the total window, as space is also used for system instructions… memories (if enabled), and internal processing… The reported space for user input is an approximation and may change dynamically.”2

Does it work at length

A window you can fill is not a window the model reasons across. There is now a benchmark for the difference.

AA-LCR — Artificial Analysis's long-context reasoning test. 100 questions over documents of 10,000 to 100,000 tokens, across company reports, industry reports, government consultations, academic papers, legal documents, marketing materials and surveys. Answers are not stated in the documents and must be inferred from several places at once.34

Its authors are direct about why simpler tests mislead.

“Simple retrieval benchmarks dramatically overestimate real-world long context capabilities.”4

XAI IS EXPLICIT THAT ONCE A REQUEST REACHES THE THRESHOLD, THE HIGHER RATE APPLIES TO ALL TOKENS IN THAT REQUEST, NOT ONLY THOSE ABOVE IT.[7] A PROMPT ONE TOKEN OVER 200K COSTS TWICE AS MUCH AS ONE A TOKEN UNDER.

Context costs memory too

If you run a model yourself, the window is not free space. Ollama documents that required RAM scales with context length multiplied by the number of parallel requests, and that four parallel requests at 2K behave like an 8K context.9

A model file that fits your card at rest may not fit it with a long conversation loaded.
